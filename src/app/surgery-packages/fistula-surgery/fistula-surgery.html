What is Fistula Surgery?

An anal fistula is a small, abnormal tunnel that forms between the inside of the anal canal and the skin near the anus - most often after an anal abscess has drained. It causes persistent discharge, discomfort, and recurring swelling, and rarely heals on its own without surgical treatment.

At Vasavi Hospitals, our surgeons offer both Laser/VAAFT (Video-Assisted Anal Fistula Treatment) - a minimally invasive, sphincter-sparing approach ideal for complex tracts - and Conventional Fistulotomy/Fistulectomy, a proven, time-tested technique for simple, low-lying fistulas, so every patient gets the approach best suited to their condition.

 Watch out for these signs

Pus or Discharge Near the Anus Recurring Pain or Swelling A Small Opening That Won't Heal Itching or Skin Irritation A History of a Previous Abscess

Why Ignoring a Fistula Is Risky

A fistula won't turn into an emergency overnight - but every month of delay lets the tract develop further. Here's what tends to happen when a fistula is left untreated.

Chronic Discharge & Skin Irritation

Ongoing pus or discharge keeps the surrounding skin irritated and uncomfortable, and rarely resolves without the tract itself being surgically addressed.

Repeat Abscess Formation

An untreated fistula can flare into a painful abscess again and again, each episode disrupting daily life and often needing urgent drainage.

More Complex Tract Branching

Over time, a simple fistula tract can branch into a more complex network, making eventual surgery more involved and recovery slower.

Recurring Discomfort

Untreated fistulas tend to flare up again and again - disrupting sitting, sleep and daily life, and often eroding confidence about the condition resolving on its own.

A single, discreet evaluation can tell you clearly how complex your fistula is and what to do next.

What to Expect During Fistula Surgery

Your Step-by-Step Care Journey

1
Consultation & Imaging

Our surgeons perform a discreet examination and, for complex tracts, an MRI fistulogram to map the fistula precisely before recommending the right technique for your case.

2
Procedure Day

Under anaesthesia, the surgeon performs either Laser/VAAFT or Conventional Fistulotomy/Fistulectomy, chosen to suit your tract's complexity. The procedure typically takes 30-60 minutes.

3
Day-Care / Short Hospital Stay

Many patients go home the same day or after one night. Pain management and clear wound-care instructions are provided before discharge.

4
Recovery & Wound Care

The surgical wound often heals gradually from the inside out over several weeks. Your surgeon will guide you on sitz baths, dressing changes and follow-up visits to support complete healing.
