What is Appendix Removal Surgery?

Appendix removal, also known as Appendectomy, is a surgical procedure performed to remove an inflamed or infected appendix - a small pouch located at the lower right side of the abdomen. When the appendix becomes swollen or bursts (a condition called appendicitis), it causes severe abdominal pain and needs immediate treatment.

At Vasavi Hospitals, our skilled surgeons perform laparoscopic and robotic-assisted appendectomy to ensure faster healing, smaller incisions and minimal discomfort. If you're experiencing sharp pain in the lower abdomen, nausea or fever, consult our specialists right away.

 Watch out for these signs

Pain Near Navel Moving Lower Right Nausea or Vomiting Fever or Loss of Appetite Pain While Coughing or Walking Abdominal Bloating

Why Delaying Appendix Surgery Is Risky

Unlike many other conditions, appendicitis can turn serious within hours, not weeks. Here's what tends to happen the longer it's left untreated.

Risk of Rupture

An inflamed appendix can burst within 48-72 hours of symptoms starting - turning a routine surgery into a surgical emergency.

Peritonitis (Spreading Infection)

A ruptured appendix spills infection into the abdominal cavity, causing widespread inflammation that needs urgent, more complex surgery.

Abscess Formation

Infection can wall itself off into a painful abscess, sometimes requiring drainage before surgery can even be performed.

Risk of Sepsis

Untreated, spreading infection can enter the bloodstream, becoming a life-threatening, hospital-intensive emergency.

Robotic Surgery (Da Vinci Xi)

The Da Vinci Xi robotic system gives surgeons 3D, high-definition vision and wristed instruments that bend and rotate far beyond the human hand - allowing unmatched precision inside the body.

3D HD vision with 10× magnification
Tremor-filtered, sub-millimetre precision
Faster recovery - shorter hospital stay
Ideal for complex or ruptured cases
Minimal post-op pain & scarring
Trusted Standard

Laparoscopic Surgery

A proven, widely used minimally invasive technique - also known as keyhole appendectomy. Small incisions, a camera, and thin instruments allow the surgeon to remove the appendix without open surgery.

Small incisions (0.5-1 cm)
Lower cost than robotic surgery
Discharge within 1-2 days
Suitable for most appendicitis cases
Covered under most insurance plans
